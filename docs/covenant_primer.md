# How Caducus Works — a Player's Primer

*The ways our covenant differs from the Ars Magica baseline, in plain terms — read this when you join. The full mechanical numbers are gathered at the end; you don't need them to start. Anything here is fair game to ask the AI-GM about, any time.*

This saga changes a number of things from "vanilla" Ars Magica — in how the covenant is governed, how its money and vis work, and who lives there. None of it affects how you *build* a magus (that's the Character Creation guide); it's about how the covenant *runs* once you're in it. Here's the short version of each.

---

## 1. How the covenant is governed

Caducus runs on **six offices named for parts of a body.** Above them sits the **Pontifex** (Aurelius, the founder) — not an officer himself, but the holder of a veto, the approver of officers' self-assignments, and the one who decides whether officers are paid. The six **officers** each own a domain of covenant life:

- **Forehead** — mundane accounts & budgets
- **Heart** — magical resources, vis, the library
- **Stomach** — internal relations; chairs the Council; verifies what officers spend
- **Groin** — income sources
- **Hand** — defence, security, the Aegis
- **Foot** — external relations; messengers; who gets to be a Guest

All Members together form the **Council**, which meets four times a year. **You will be offered one of these offices early on** — Aurelius delegates them at the founding — and running it well (proposing a worthwhile yearly **project** for it) is a real part of play.

Above the covenant, the Order has its own **Tribunal** — the regional gathering of magi that judges Hermetic crimes, hears disputes, and votes on matters like new covenants' recognition. Caducus sits in the **Novgorod Tribunal**, and its own standing there is still being settled: our founding is Rhine-sponsored, in a region Novgorod has its own claims to, so our voting **sigils** (a magus's seat at the Tribunal) start out contested rather than automatically held. **Foot** carries the Charter duty of Tribunal envoy — representing us there and to the Order's traveling Redcaps and Quaesitors (the Order's own investigators and law-keepers). Depth beyond this — how a case actually unfolds, what a contested sigil means for you specifically, whether Ex Miscellanea is open to a hedge-tradition character — is ask-the-AI-GM territory, any time. Underneath the story, the wider Order's politics — who backs whom, which blocs whip a vote, how a Gild or House leans on a given issue — is run by a live simulation the AI-GM consults, not freeform improv; your own magus isn't tracked by it (that side of you is played, not modeled), but a Reputation you earn will show on your character sheet's Politics tab.

## 2. The year, and how you plan it

We play **one in-game year at a time, as a block.** All of a year's stories run at once, and you plan the *next* year throughout the current one — the AI keeps asking you what your magus and your office intend to do, and gives you a **projection** of what resources you'll have to work with, so you're planning against real figures, not guesses.

**Your magus has four season-slots a year**, and you plan all four together (the whole year is in play at once). Most seasons are quiet work — lab, study, writing, vis extraction — one activity each. A *story* may or may not cost a slot: a **short adventure (up to a month in the fiction) is a "distraction"** that costs **no slot** — you keep the full result of your planned work, and may instead take Adventure XP for the adventure. Only a **long story (more than a month)** eats the season. So short stories layer on for free; only a long expedition spends a slot. (The full detail, the year's calendar, and your own figures live on **[Plan Your Season](../covenant/plan.html)**.)

**Filing a season's plan usually takes effect right away** — you don't wait for anyone's approval to start the work. A Time-Storyguide may still review it afterward, and if something's wrong (a factual gap, a conflict with someone else's plan, or similar), they can **flag it**: you'll be told why on the forum, and can simply re-file that season with a corrected plan — a flagged season never blocks you from planning it again.

Two moments anchor the year:
- **A reconciliation, partway through** — the AI gathers everyone's planned projects and their costs into one thread and pings the officers, so you can fit your plans to the budget and to each other.
- **The Spring Council, at the turn of the year** — played out in character. The Council approves the year's budget and handles any other business between the magi.

**One rule worth knowing up front:** the *proportions* of the budget — how much vis, silver, and service each office gets — are **set a year ahead and can't be changed for the year they govern.** The reason is simple: you can't plan a project without knowing its budget, so the shares have to be fixed before anyone plans against them. So when the Council re-cuts the shares, the change takes effect the year *after*. (For the first two years the founder's allocation stands; you get your first real say at the Spring 1221 Council, for 1222.)

## 3. Money and vis

- **Each office gets a yearly budget** — a share of the covenant's vis, its silver, and its service-seasons — and works within it. Unspent budget does **not** roll over.
- **Each office pays its own running costs out of that budget.** Your office's allocation isn't pure spending money — it first has to cover the upkeep of the covenfolk and assets *your office commands* (the Hand feeds and pays its grogs; the Heart, its scribes); what's left is your **discretionary** budget for projects. So if you take on more people, the cost comes out of *your* budget, not the whole covenant's surplus — and you can't overspend your allocation without going to the Council for an Additional Budget. (The magi's own upkeep is a covenant-wide cost, not charged to any office; and an established **income source** pays for itself and just hands the covenant its net profit.)
- **There is no vis tax.** The covenant doesn't skim a tithe off your vis. The only vis obligation is the officer-salary formula below.
- **Officers are paid** a tenth of what their projects actually spent, in vis, at year's end — the Pontifex decides whether to pay it.
- **A reserve of vis is always kept back** for the Aegis and never spent down.
- **"Universal Vis."** For *budgeting*, all vis is treated as one interchangeable currency ("Universal Vis", or UV) so that a budget can be written without naming exact Arts; it's converted to real pawns of specific Arts when actually spent. You'll see budgets quoted in UV.
- **What's yours vs. the covenant's:** vis and items you make **entirely with your own time and resources** stay your private property; anything made with covenant vis, covenant resources, or a service-season belongs to Caducus. Discovered vis belongs to the covenant; you keep a finder's fee for a self-funded discovery (none if found during service or a covenant-funded expedition). Using the shared Aegis, lab, library, and provisions doesn't make your work the covenant's. **Register it with the Forehead within 30 days of making it** — unregistered private property is presumed Covenant Property if it's ever audited or subject to Confiscation (Charter s8.4).
- **House Mercere's vis loans, promised in your founding letter, are a real standing option — not just a line from home.** Ask to borrow vis any time a Redcap visit window is open (the twice-yearly supply-ship schedule, above): standard terms are **20%/year interest, minimum one pawn, five-year term.** A bigger ask than the standard cap allows gets negotiated instead — the Redcap network settles it at the following visit.
- **Durenmar's Great Library — your founding letter calls it "our most precarious foundation" — is a real, working relationship too.** The covenant can request texts (summae, tractatus, lab texts) the Library holds, and donate your own written work to it for study credit toward advancement. Ask the AI-GM what's currently on offer, or to make a request or donation, any time — **Heart** tracks the relationship.
- **Want more from your laboratory, or a ceremonial casting chamber?** Construction is a covenant matter, not something a magus builds alone — ask for it in the app, and whichever office handles laboratory upkeep commissions it out of their budget.
- **Vis doesn't just show up — it's harvested from real sources, on their own schedule.** Some sources only yield during a narrow **harvest window** (a saint's day, a lunar phase, a season), and working one too often or out of turn — or a rival covenant working one Caducus holds — is a real, resolvable dispute, not just a rumor. **Trading and haggling** (with a merchant, a rival covenant, anyone) can be a genuine multi-round negotiation, not a single take-it-or-leave-it offer, and a magus can **spont-craft** a mundane object with Rego magic instead of a craftsman's hands. The wider mundane craft-and-guild economy — workshops, journeyman standing, guild contracts — exists mostly at the GM level; ask if your magus wants to engage with it directly.

## 4. The people of the covenant — read this, and see the safety note

Caducus is not just magi. It is a working household on a hard frontier, and that includes people in very different conditions:

- **The covenant owns enslaved people.** This is a fact of the saga, not background colour. Thirteenth-century Finland had slaves, and this covenant holds some; they do unpaid labour and have no standing. The saga treats this as a **live moral matter** — something the fiction will confront, not gloss over. If that is something you'd rather not engage with, or want handled a certain way, **that is exactly what our lines-and-veils and the X-card are for** — raise it at session zero or any time (see the safety brief, and ask the GM). Nobody is expected to be comfortable with it by default.
- **The covenant also has free covenfolk and hired labourers**, and a close arrangement with the local **Koroinen** clan for labour and goods.
- **The North is poor and cold.** Mundane work yields less here than in the lush South, so the covenant has to spend more to feed and house itself than a southern covenant would — which is part of why the budget is tight and the frontier vis matters so much.

## 5. Rolls and Ease Factors

- **Any check your magus attempts — climbing, persuading someone, recalling a piece of lore, sneaking past a watch — is measured against an Ease Factor (EF): the number your roll needs to beat.** The AI doesn't pick that number by feel. It's built from a **base difficulty** (a fixed ladder, below) plus any **named circumstances** that make the moment easier or harder than the plain, everyday case — poor light, a hostile audience, working in a language you barely speak, and so on.
- **Crossing a language barrier is one of those circumstances, and it's a real mechanic, not just an EF footnote.** Your magus's Language score doesn't add to a die roll — it measures how well you actually communicate, from barely pointing-and-grunting up to speaking as well as a native. Two people who share only a related tongue still manage, at a penalty that grows with the distance between the two languages — a different dialect of your own tongue costs the least, a language from the same family costs more, and past a certain remove (Finnish against a Germanic or Slavic tongue, say) there's no shared ground at all without an interpreter or a common third language like Latin. And when the conversation isn't happening in a tongue you're truly fluent in, any social Ability you use through it is capped at how well you can actually communicate, not your full score — the same limit the rules put on commanding a revived corpse in a language you barely know. Ask the AI-GM how well your magus and anyone else can actually talk to each other, any time.
- **A "what do I know about X?" question is a roll too, and if your magus doesn't know something, the game tells you plainly rather than making something up.** The AI routes the question to whichever Ability actually covers it (Order of Hermes Lore for the Order itself; Area Lore, Church Lore, or the like for a mundane place or person) — never the most magus-sounding Ability you happen to have. A roll your magus has no business making at all (an Ability he's never studied, when the subject demands one) simply doesn't happen: you're told your magus has no idea, not handed a guess dressed up as fact. And a **failed** roll reveals nothing of the withheld answer, not even a partial or a hint of tone.
- **The difficulty ladder**, roughly: **Trivial** (barely worth rolling) → **Simple** → **Easy** → **Average** (the everyday case) → **Hard** → **Very Hard** → **Impressive** → **Remarkable** → **Almost Impossible** (even a master succeeds only when lucky).
- **Circumstance modifiers come from a fixed, growable list, not a number invented on the spot** — the same situation always carries the same modifier, whoever's running the scene. When a roll's difficulty includes any, the OOC receipt itemizes it (base + each named circumstance) so you can see exactly why it's harder or easier than the average case, not just a bare final number.
- **If a situation genuinely has no matching entry yet, the AI can't invent one mid-scene** — the list only grows through the storyguide. You may occasionally see a roll go through on the base difficulty alone while that gets added for next time.

## 6. Casting, Parma, and when a fight happens

- **Casting a spell is a real roll, not a narrative flourish.** Every cast computes a **Casting Total** — your relevant Art scores, plus Stamina, plus the local aura, minus any encumbrance — against the spell's own level. The AI shows you the math and the die; the story follows the result, never the other way around.
- **A spell that outlasts the scene it was cast in — Sun, Moon, Year duration, or a standing effect from an enchanted item — is a real, tracked thing, not narration that quietly fades from memory.** The AI keeps a live record of what's actually still active on you, on the covenant, and on anyone or anywhere else (a Longevity Ritual, a ward on the sanctum, the Aegis itself), and checks it before ruling on anything the effect could matter to. Ask the AI-GM what's currently active on you or on a place, any time.
- **Penetration is what lets a spell affect someone who resists it.** Most people, animals, and ordinary objects have no Magic Resistance at all, so a spell simply affects them. Against a target that *does* resist — chiefly another magus, or a creature with a magical Might — your spell needs enough **Penetration** (built from your Casting Total and the spell's level, and boosted by things like an Arcane Connection or the Penetration Ability) to overcome that target's resistance, or nothing happens.
- **An Arcane Connection (AC) is a physical link to a person, place, or thing** — a lock of hair, a bit of home soil, anything that was genuinely part of or intimately tied to the target — and holding one gives a huge boost to Penetration against that specific target, no matter the distance between you. A found AC fades over time as it goes stale; spending a lab season to **fix** one (a real activity, costing a season and some vis) makes it good indefinitely.
- **Spell Mastery** rewards a spell you've cast again and again: a **Mastery Score** adds to your Casting Total whenever you cast that one spell, and reaching a new mastery level lets you pick a **special ability** for it — casting it faster or more quietly, striking several targets at once, or, for a Vim specialist, using it to unravel or seize control of someone else's magic. Ask the AI-GM what abilities your mastered spells have picked up.
- **Vim magic can act on magic itself, not just the ordinary five Arts' business.** A Vim spell can unravel, redirect, or seize control of an existing magical effect — another caster's spell, an enchanted item's working — under its own set of guidelines rather than the usual Range/Duration/Target ladder. Ask the AI-GM what a specific Vim working against a target effect could accomplish.
- **Parma Magica is a magus's own shield, and it's not something you can leave off by accident.** Every magus keeps up a base Magic Resistance from their Parma — roughly five points of resistance per point of Parma Score, on top of ordinary Form resistance — that blocks *any* Hermetic magic aimed at them, friendly healing as much as a hostile curse, unless they deliberately **lower it**. You can ask the AI to check your own Parma status (raised, or currently suppressed) at any time.
- **Certamen is the Order's own formal magical duel** — two magi pit chosen Arts against each other in a ritualized contest, resolved with real rolls (and real vis, if either side spends it to boost their total), never narrated freehand. It's how the Order settles a dispute between magi without swords — and it's also how your **Gauntlet**, the ceremony that ends apprenticeship, can be resolved: a parens or Quaesitor may choose to test a candidate with a certamen rather than simply declaring them ready.

A fight — a skirmish, a raid, a duel — is resolved for real, not just narrated: every blow, spell, and check is rolled and computed the moment it happens, the same discipline as any other roll in the saga. You'll see the mechanical result (a wound, whether a strike lands) as it's rolled, and the story is written around it, never the other way around.

The **Hand's turb** — the covenant's own guards and soldiers — is Caducus's fighting strength. When several fighters (the turb, a raiding party, any group) fight together as one unit rather than as individuals, that unit has its own morale, tracked separately from any single fighter's wounds: a unit that's taken a beating can come out of a fight **shaken** — still fighting, but worse for it — or **fleeing** outright, and stays that way until someone successfully rallies them. It's a real, rollable consequence, not a narrative flourish — ask the AI-GM what state a group is in if it matters to your scene.

A full-scale **battle** (armies, not a skirmish) works the same way at a larger scale: the losing side's commander can falter under the strain of a lost engagement, and a faltering army needs rallying too, just like a smaller group would.

## 7. Getting hurt, and getting better

A wound, an illness, or plain exhaustion are all real, tracked conditions — not just narration. **Wounds** come in bands (light, medium, heavy) that penalize everything you do until they heal, on a real recovery timeline running from a week to a full season depending on severity; **fatigue** is shorter-lived and recovers with rest. **Disease and poison** work the same way — a real progression through stages, not an instant either-or, and a serious case can reach a **Crisis**: the sharpest, most dangerous point in its course, where the outcome swings hardest one way or the other. Some Flaws (**Susceptible to** a particular disease or poison) make a magus especially easy to afflict in the first place. Your covenant's medical staff matters: the game automatically pulls in the best available Chirurgy or Medicine care while your magus recovers. **Death is never silently finalized** — a fatal wound, a fatal disease crisis, or terminal old age flags for the GM's acknowledgment rather than simply ending your character off-screen (see the safety brief for how that moment is handled at the table). Ask the AI-GM about your magus's current condition any time.

## 8. Advancement — growing your magus

Most seasons your magus does something that makes them better: study from a book, get taught, write a book of your own, or invent a new spell in the lab. **Working from a book takes real fluency in whatever language it's written in** — reading one needs a Language score of at least 4 in its tongue, and writing one of your own needs at least 5 (nearly everything written at Caducus is in Latin); short of that, the book isn't yet something you can use. **A season buys progress in one thing, not several** — pick a single activity. Studying, teaching, and writing all raise a specific Ability or Art; inventing a spell is its own kind of work, and it is neither XP nor a roll — your lab work each season overshoots the spell's level by some margin, and you bank that margin season after season until the banked total reaches the level itself. A spell comfortably within your reach is finished in one season; one that only just fits takes several. **Drawing raw vis into your own Art carries real risk** — it's not a free shortcut, and a bad roll can cost more than the season did. Training an apprentice is a real seasonal duty too, with real consequences for neglecting it. Ask the AI-GM about the numbers behind any specific activity, any time.

**Adventure XP, and the choice behind it.** A story that runs no longer than a month is the "distraction" from Section 2: it costs you no season-slot, and it leaves you a choice — keep the full result of the quiet work you had planned, **or** take **Adventure XP** for the story instead. You cannot have both, and the choice is yours to make afterward, once you know how each turned out. (A story that runs *longer* than a month is the other case: it eats into that season's work in proportion to the time you lost to it.) Adventure XP arrives as a **Source Quality** the storyguide sets by how much there genuinely was to learn — a routine errand is worth less than a disaster you barely walked away from. You then split it across what the story actually exercised, under two limits worth knowing before you plan around it: **no more than five of those points may go into any one Ability or Art**, and while an **Ability** counts even if your magus only used it off-stage — on the road there, say — an **Art** counts only if you actually used it **on-stage**, in play. Off-stage Arts are refused, so a season of travel does not quietly advance your Creo.

## 9. Warping, Twilight, and Confidence

Working with power leaves a mark. **Warping Points** quietly accrue — from a strong ongoing magical effect, from a botch, from a Longevity Ritual — usually slowly, sometimes suddenly; enough of them raise your **Warping Score**, and your sheet shows both numbers. Take on enough at once and your magus checks for **Twilight**: a real, mechanically-resolved episode where your magus effectively steps out of ordinary play while it works itself out. **Nothing lifts a Twilight on a timer** — no number of seasons passing ends it, and your magus cannot file plans while it lasts. It ends when the storyguide resolves it, so if yours is in Twilight and you want them back in play, say so on the forum and ask. It's a consequence of working with power, never a punishment for playing wrong, and Final Twilight is never something the game springs on you without warning. **Aging** happens every year and can bring a real crisis — illness, growing decrepitude — as your magus gets older; the AI walks you through it when it comes up.

**Confidence** is two different numbers, and your sheet shows both. Your **Confidence Score** is how much you can bring to bear on any one roll; your **Confidence Points** are the reserve you spend from, earned through play and gone for good once spent. A point adds +3 to a roll, and you may decide to spend it *after* seeing the dice, once you know the result you'd otherwise be stuck with. Three limits are worth knowing before you count on it: you can never spend more points on a single roll than your Confidence **Score**; you cannot spend it to rescue a roll that **botched** (an ordinary bad roll, yes — a botch, no); and you cannot spend it on a roll that stands in for a **whole season's work**, such as studying from vis. Ask the AI-GM what your current reserve is, any time.

**Not all vis is safe to use.** Vis tainted by the Infernal comes in three grades, and — this is the part worth getting right before you spend any — **only one of the three is actually stronger than clean vis.** The mildest grade (*infesta*) behaves like ordinary vis in the casting; what it changes is that any botch it was involved in comes out nastier than it should have. The middle grade (*sordida*) genuinely is more potent, pushing a casting further than the same amount of clean vis would, and that is exactly what makes it the most tempting and the most dangerous: it also piles on botch dice that no Virtue or spell mastery can reduce away. The worst grade (*prava*) is not a bonus at all — any roll it touches counts as a zero and goes straight to a botch check, which is very nearly a promise of disaster rather than a risk of one.

Contact alone has a price separate from any casting: spending a season or more working with tainted vis of any grade, or simply carrying it about, imposes a **Personality Trait** on your magus rather than Warping — one that fades once the vis is out of their life, unless they've indulged it, in which case it stays. Warping Points come specifically from *studying* from the worst grade, one per pawn. Your magus's own magic cannot detect any of this — an examination shows plain vis, and most magi have never heard the grades distinguished at all — so treat the paragraph you just read as table-level knowledge rather than something your character necessarily knows. What you *will* see is practical: the app prints the grade on any tainted lot you hold, on your own sheet, so you are never asked to spend blind.

## 10. Your familiar

Many magi eventually find and bond with a **familiar** — a beast touched by real magic that becomes, in a very literal sense, the closest companion a magus will ever have. It isn't a pet or a servant: a familiar keeps its own will and can't be commanded, and finding and befriending one worth bonding is real story material, not a shopping trip. Binding it is a genuine lab activity — a season in your seasonal plan, the same weight of work as inventing a spell or opening an item — and it also forges three magical **cords** between you:

- **The Golden Cord** protects your spellcasting: it reduces the botch dice you roll on any cast (though never all the way to zero).
- **The Silver Cord** strengthens your mind: it bonuses rolls involving your Personality Traits and rolls to resist mental influence, and lets your familiar attempt, once a day, to shake your mind free if it's ever overcome by something else.
- **The Bronze Cord** strengthens your body: it bonuses Soak, healing, holding your breath or otherwise resisting deprivation, and resisting the effects of aging (though not fatigue).

Each cord is scored 0-5, and you divide your binding season's own Lab Total across all three however you like — a stronger cord costs more of that total, so you're choosing a balance, not maxing out everything at once. A later season can strengthen the bond further, raising any cord's score (never lowering one).

A bound familiar and its magus each gain the Virtue True Friend and come to regard each other as Loyal — and each becomes a standing Arcane Connection to the other, with neither needing to overcome the other's resistance to affect the other with their own magic. Over the years together, a familiar picks up what its magus studies and can even serve as a lab assistant. You can only bond one familiar at a time. Later, in the lab, you can also invest real magical powers directly into the bond itself — an effect that touches you, your familiar, or both — without it causing the ongoing Warping a permanent invested effect normally would.

**What happens if one of you dies.** A familiar ages and dies alongside its magus, and never simply of old age while its magus lives — its sudden, unexplained death is itself a warning that its magus is in real danger. Should the magus die first, the familiar suffers a genuine shock that can kill it outright, and even if it survives, it's left permanently and visibly diminished. Should the familiar die first, the magus is left with a real, lasting grief that can last months or years. This isn't glossed over as flavor text — losing a familiar, like losing a magus, is a genuine table moment (see the safety brief for how that's handled).

## 11. Enchantment — making magic items

Beyond casting and inventing spells, a magus can bind magic permanently into an object — a talisman, a charged item, a lesser enchantment. It's a real, buildable lab activity, the same kind of work as inventing a spell, not just flavor text for a wealthy covenant. Ask the AI-GM what it would take to open an item and invest an effect in it.

## 12. Beyond the ordinary — Mystery Cults

Some magi walk a further path: a **Mystery Cult** initiation can open Supernatural Virtues, a House's own secret arts (Bjornaer shapeshifting, Merinita's faerie ties, Verditius's enchanting boosts, Criamon's Enigma), or wider traditions like Theurgy, Spirit Magic, Alchemy, Astrology, and Divination. These are real, reachable late-game content, not lore dressing — if your magus is drawn to one, ask the AI-GM what pursuing it would actually take.

## 13. Reputation

A Reputation is what people who've never met your magus have already heard about them — a score, a content (what it's for), and a type: **Local** (anyone who lives nearby), **Ecclesiastical** (standing within the Church), or **Hermetic** (standing within the Order). Someone who's heard of you doesn't necessarily know what to make of you: a high Reputation can precede you just as easily for the worse as for the better, and can even make it harder to convince a stranger that you're who you say you are.

You don't need a Virtue to start one. **Any noteworthy deed done in front of witnesses who'll talk is enough** — surviving a fight with something fearsome, preaching a heretical sermon, a magus who gives the covenant's vis away at Tribunal — and from there, further noteworthy actions in the same vein build on it, the same way practice raises an Ability. **Shedding a bad Reputation takes real, deliberate effort:** you have to do something spectacular enough to start a competing one, then keep earning it credit until it overtakes the old one — at which point you can choose to spend further gains knocking the old Reputation down instead of raising the new one further. Most magi are best served carrying no more than two Reputations at once; more than that just muddies what people actually think they know about you.

Ask the AI-GM about your magus's current Reputation(s), or whether a scene you just played through was Reputation-worthy, any time — anything already on record shows up on your sheet in the app.

## 14. When a dispute goes to mundane law

Not every conflict is settled by magic or persuasion in the moment — some go to a real mundane court, guild tribunal, or similar hearing, and when they do, it's a genuine mechanical contest, not just narration. Both sides argue their case (Communication plus a relevant legal or logic Ability), and the higher roll wins, swung by real circumstances: a credible majority of witnesses helps you, a better Reputation helps you, a defendant who's actually innocent gets a boost, and a prior ruling in your favor at the guild level can carry over into town court. **Your magus's Gift works against you here, not for you:** if you speak for yourself before the court, being Gifted costs you a real penalty to your case — worse still if your Gift is Blatant — the same mistrust that unsettles ordinary people around a magus in any social setting, now playing out in a room that can rule against you. Having someone else speak on your behalf avoids that specific penalty. Ask the AI-GM about a dispute your magus is caught up in, any time.

## The numbers (for when you want the specifics)

You don't need these to play; the AI applies them and will quote them on request. They live canonically in the covenant **Charter** and the saga's House Rules.

- **Offices:** seven — Pontifex (not an officer) + Forehead, Heart, Stomach, Groin, Hand, Foot. Members may hold up to three.
- **Service owed:** **Members 1 season/year, Guests 2.** (You begin as a Guest; you become a full Member at the Spring 1221 Council.)
- **Officer salary (dual-currency):** **1/10 of the project expenditure that officer oversaw and completed that year**, in the completion year, at the Pontifex's discretion. **Mundane project spend → £ salary** (paid in pounds); **vis project spend → vis salary** (in UV, **minimum 0.25 UV**, paid from Concrete Vis in stock, **first-come-first-served**). Not a tax; not a ceiling.
- **Strategic Vis Reserve:** **5 pawns of Rego or Vim** held at all times for the Aegis.
- **No rollover** of unspent budget; **no vis tax.**
- **Universal Vis exchange** (Charter §6.4), stock assessed the first day of Winter (for budget/salary only — your concrete-vis plan costs are valued **when you submit them**, first-come-first-served on in-stock vis):
  - In stock: **1 UV = 2 Technique** pawns, **1 UV = 4 Form** pawns.
  - Not in stock: **1 UV = 1 Technique** pawn, **1 UV = 2 Form** pawns.
- **Supply ships:** two visits/year — **Oculus in early Spring** (via Falke's Baltic-thaw ritual) and **Waddenzee in early Autumn** (pre-ice). Redcaps ride along; **Foot** oversees them.
