# The Gilds of the Rhine — and the quarrel of young and old


*Every magus you'll play came from the Rhine Tribunal, and the Rhine runs on Gilds. Your character almost certainly belongs to one and holds a view on the great quarrel below — so this page is the common knowledge you need to choose a stance in character creation.*

## What a Gild is

A **Gild** is a Rhine magus's political affiliation — **purely political** (it has no magical function), tree-named in the German fashion. The six long-standing Gilds are the **Oak** (Eichengilde), **Linden** (Lindengilde), **Ash** (Eschengilde), **Hawthorn** (Weissdorngilde), **Elder** (Holundergilde), and **Apple** (Apfelgilde). A magus belongs to **one** Gild; membership is open and public — no one approves it — but *leaving* a Gild is a risky, watched act.

*(The Gild system is ordinary Rhine custom from the rulebooks. The **Birch Gild** and the two doctrines below — and how sharply the young and the old are at odds in this saga — are Caducus's own additions to that custom.)*

## The quarrel: the Elm versus the Saplings

The Rhine's defining fight is between the **gerontes** — the centuries-old elders who hold the vis, the libraries, and the votes — and the **young magi** who feel shut out of all three. Two opposed doctrines name the two sides, and **every character takes a position on each.**

**The Elm of Hermes** — the elders' creed. The Order is a great elm: the oldest magi are the deep **roots** that give the whole tree its stability, knowledge, and power. Its three branches:
- *the Old Are Wise* — centuries of study make the elders the best leaders;
- *the Old Protect* — they wield the strongest shield and guard Bonisagus's legacy;
- *the Old Deserve* — they built the Order by sacrifice, so it is theirs by right.

Critics answer that the elm is prone to disease and to death, its greedy roots cracking foundations and starving the smaller plants around it.

**Ashes to Saplings** — the reformers' creed. Picture a birch sprouting from a burned forest: renewal comes from **clearing the deadwood** of outdated structures, and it is the young saplings — not the elders clinging to the past — who carry the Order forward. In practice that means a shared, freely-copied library; collective ownership of vis sources; centralized early teaching; and rotation between masters rather than lifelong service to one. Critics answer that the birch is short-lived beside the sturdy elm, and that too-rapid change severs centuries-old roots.

## The Birch Gild

The **Birch Gild** is the seventh, radical Gild — the organized vehicle of *Ashes to Saplings*, led by **Lucilla ex Tytalus** (her founding "Warning" is one of the [Founding Letters](founding_letters.html)). It holds that the elders will not yield freely, so the young must **actively pressure** them. Its platform: a centralized, redcap-maintained library open to all, with the right to consult any covenant's texts; **collective ownership of vis sources**; no single lifelong master; and an end to age-based political power (no inheriting or gifting voting sigils), plus the secret ballot. It draws the young and the unruly and has real support in several Rhine covenants — though Lucilla herself holds no covenant seat, by principle.

## Where the old Gilds stand

- **Oak** — the Elm's chief champion; the elders' party; fiercely against *Ashes*.
- **Linden** — *"prune, don't uproot"*: the reformist middle, for moderate redistribution. **Aurelius, our Pontifex, is a Linden** — the same enemy as the Birchers (entrenched elder power), but a gentler cure.
- **Ash** — partly with the Elm: the elders best defend the Rhine against the Order of Odin, and Ash fears youthful upheaval weakening that defence.
- **Hawthorn** — skeptical of the Elm, indifferent to *Ashes*; the wilderness comes first.
- **Elder** — neutral in the quarrel; cares only for preserving the fae.
- **Apple** — leans toward the Elm, but favours a gradual, inheritance-like handover of power.

## What this means for your character

A Rhine peregrinator almost certainly belongs to a Gild and holds **a stance on the Elm and on Ashes** — choose both when you build, and your stance can be ardent, weary, opportunistic, or genuinely torn. **If your magus belongs to a Gild, they have 4 build points of private assets** (per the list in *Covenants* p.5) that represent its benefits — and the Gild's members may later expect their **solidarity**.

Caducus itself spans the whole quarrel: founded under a Linden moderate, backed by elders far away, and just the sort of raw frontier outpost a young Bircher might read as either an exile to be endured or a beachhead to be seized. Where your magus sits in all this — and what they hope to *do* about it — is one of the richest seeds for their story.
