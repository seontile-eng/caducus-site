# The Founding Letters of Caducus
*Player-facing required reading. The three letters every prospective peregrinator of Caducus received in 1218 — Aurelius's invitation, Lucilla's rebuttal, and Aurelius's letter of acceptance and logistics. They are in-character documents: Aurelius is recruiting, Lucilla is agitating, and both are writing two years before the covenant exists. Read them for the saga's premise, its politics, and the offer on the table — and bring your questions to the GM.*

---

## I — The Invitation
**February 10th, 1218 — Aurelius ex Bonisagus, to the Apprentices of the Rhine**
*An Invitation to Caducus in Spring 1220*

Esteemed Soon-to-be Covenant-mates,

have you reflected on how a river flows? Its waters change, carving new paths through the landscape, yet the river itself remains. So, too, with our beloved Order. I invite you to converge with me and like-minded friends at Caducus, a covenant to be founded in Turku, at the very frontier of the Order.

You, who traverse the spaces between the elms, understand that the ancient trees of the Rhine's heartlands, though majestic, can cast shadows on new saplings. Do the venerable covenants demand much of your time, folding your unique contributions into the preservation of the same? At Caducus, we listen for a different harmony.

Here, service is a shared melody of manifold voices. We ask for but one season of members' time per annum – though two for peregrinators – granting you the freedom to explore your own research, nurture your projects, and follow your unique path. Is not a magus akin to a bird soaring on unseen currents, best when unencumbered? We honor the belief that magic—and the magus—is not a static entity but a dynamic force, in perpetual dialogue with the world. Your time is a precious thread in the loom of destiny, meant to weave your own transformation, not merely to reinforce the tapestries of others.

Moreover, Caducus offers opportunities for growth. Once you have intertwined your journey with ours in peregrination, the path to equal membership lies open before you. This is not a maze with hidden doors but a clear, reciprocal recognition of value. Can true unity exist without mutual respect and shared purpose? At Caducus, membership is not a layering but a circle—ever expanding, ever inclusive.

Consider the land of Suomi, as its inhabitants know it. Surely a paradise, where vis flows abundantly, untamed streams after the thaw. Unlike the heartlands, where resources are scarce and guarded like treasures in a dragon's lair, the environs of Caducus offer raw, unclaimed magical potential. Is it not the dream of every magus to tap into such boundless energy, to shape it according to their vision?

Our covenant provides the fertile ground for your aspirations. Excellent laboratories await your touch to stir them. Our books will form a living forest of knowledge, its leaves whispering the secrets of both ancient roots and new blossoms. And the subsidies of mundane resources put aside for Caducus are plentiful, promising freedom from distractions. Does not the gardener cultivate best when the soil is rich and the tools sharp?

Finally, there is the allure of the frontier itself. Caducus stands at the boundary of the Order, in a realm where definitions blur and new possibilities emerge like dawn breaking over uncharted lands. Here, the old rules bend, and the margins become the canvas for innovation. Are we not, each of us, seekers standing at the edge of the known world, yearning to step into the mysteries beyond?

We offer not the stasis of tradition but the fluidity of morphosis. If you seek a place to explore new horizons, to cultivate your magic in a space where nothing is fixed and everything is interconnected, then let Caducus be your next journey. Here, the future is still being written, and the quill rests in your hands. Send me your willingness along with credentials that might facilitate my choice.

With earnest anticipation,
**Aurelius ex Bonisagus**
*Founder of Caducus*

## II — The Warning
**March 20th, 1218 — Lucilla ex Tytalus, for the Birch Gild**
*Be Wary of Aurelius' Invitation!*

Aurelius,

your grand invitation to Caducus, adorned with the allure of opportunity and freedom, is nothing more than a veiled attempt to siphon the vigor of our young to the distant reaches of Terra Fennorum. Meanwhile, the gerontes of the Rhine Tribunal sit entrenched, reaping the benefits of our labor without challenge. Let us dispense with illusions: this founding is not about new horizons or untapped potential; it is a calculated move to divert the restless minds and hands of our generation, ensuring the old order remains unthreatened.

Why should we invest resources to build laboratories and libraries so far from the heart of the Rhine when our own tribunal suffers under the weight of scarcity, stagnation and conflict? Or have you forgotten the confrontations at Durenmar, where younger magi were denied the resources rightfully theirs, even after seasons of obligatory service? What about the standoff at Fengheld, where we, the disenfranchised, stood firm against those who would hoard vis and knowledge–and won? Or the quiet rebellion at Roznov, where young journeymen refused to enter contracts amounting to serfdom? These are not mere anecdotes; the new peeks through the cracks of the old.

Aristotle says: "He who cannot be a good follower cannot be a good leader". Well, we both know your credentials as a follower, Aurelius, given that Caducus is your ample reward for services rendered to old Gregorius. But what about true leadership? Your shadow looming over the covenant will be the shadow of the gerontes also. What freedom is this?

Moreover, the land you so highly praise—the "promising frontier" of Terra Fennorum—is fraught with peril you conveniently ignore. The Curonians raid from the south, the Svear press from the West, and the Rutheni advance from the East. Even notwithstanding the fabular Odinites (did you forget your masters' own bestiary so soon?), the region is a tinderbox of conflict. As a final straw, the Dominion's influence encroaches from West, East, and South, threatening to smother the magical paradise you claim awaits. Do you expect us to turn a blind eye to these realities?

And what of the plentiful vis? Do you truly believe it lies unclaimed, waiting for you like fruit on a pregnant branch? The local inhabitants—who have known the land long before our Order's lustful gaze—will not stand idly by as we seize what is theirs. You will be seen not as explorers or scholars, but as invaders and plunderers, which is what you are.

So, to those who are tempted by Aurelius' offer of Caducus, I say this: by all means, join this venture if you wish. But know that it is no haven, it is a battleground. Prepare to fight the harsh environment as well as the gerontocracy that Aurelius serves. Join, but fear the right things, for the right motive, in the right manner, and at the right time. Amass the resources you need, sharpen your skills, and then turn them against those who would keep you in chains: the gerontes of the Rhine and those, like Aurelius, who seek to lead you astray.

Freedom is not given, but taken. Turn oaks into ashes, ashes into saplings, saplings into a forest, and together, redefine what it means to be magi of the Rhine. (To learn how, read my magnum opus, *Virtuous Potentacy*. I have donated a copy to the covenant. Aurelius will surely add it to the library now that you know.)

**Lucilla ex Tytalus**
*Peregrinator of the Rhine*
*For the Birch Gild*

## III — Acceptance & Logistics
**June 1st, 1218 — Aurelius of Bonisagus, to the accepted peregrinators**

Esteemed Visitors,

it is with great anticipation and humility that I, Aurelius of Bonisagus, inform you of your acceptance as peregrinators to our nascent covenant, Caducus. From among many worthy applicants, I sought to form a harmonious and capable fellowship. You will find an enclosed appendix listing the names, talents, current locations, and preliminary travel plans of your fellows, should you wish to weave your fates together.

In the tapestry of time, a year may seem both fleeting and eternal–but the early bird may catch unexpected prey, or at least avoid dangers! On the 21st of January 1220, with the beneficence of Falke, we shall embark from Oculus Septentrionalis, which has graciously lent us three sturdy cogs for our maiden voyage. The winds of preparation now fill your sails; use this interlude to ready yourselves as best you can.

Oculus has also promised to waive seasons of compulsory service for the year 1219 to any peregrinators if I deem their projects for the year as preparation for Caducus. You may use this opportunity to conduct four seasons of work that is beneficial to the covenant as a whole, and any tangible products will become the covenant's property. I will suggest some avenues below, but you may also present your own for my approval. I encourage you to discuss and coordinate preparations with each other. Should you wish to pursue your own projects during 1219, two seasons of compulsory service for Oculus will be expected, so only two will remain for yourselves.

Our journey to Turku should span approximately fourteen days, barring the intervention of your own magical ingenuity. As with any voyage into the unknown, we must be vigilant. Pirates lurk where law wanes, storms and ice floes gather unbidden, faeries weave their illusions, the cold wind bites into the bone, and unseen rocks lie in wait beneath the waves. Hedge magicians may roam the shores, and our mundane crew may resent our Gifts. Embracing the unexpected is the crucible in which wisdom is forged.

I have walked the grounds of our future home—a modest peninsula nestled at the confluence of two rivers with steep banks, defensible on two fronts yet open on a third, much like the balance we seek between isolation and communion. The aura there is unusually potent, a beacon for the mystical, though other arcane qualities remain largely shrouded and ripe for your investigations.

The surrounding region teems with people. Farmsteads lie scattered like seeds, striking the earth with primitive implements. Hunters prey on abundant fauna in endless forests, fishermen haunt the nearby rapids, and traders from faraway lands ply the rivers that extend deep inland. Two peoples – the largely pagan Finns and the largely Christian Swedes – intermingle here without, as far as I can tell, central leadership. This is in spite of the best efforts of the Catholic Church, and lesser efforts by the Orthodox. The Swedes largely abide by their men, but amongst the Finns, the womenfolk hold some sway.

The fecund supernatural landscape is as yet unknown, but a few features immediately stand out upon interviewing a local. A plethora of minor faeries and deities; peculiar sorts of "lords" bearing the title of "kuningas"; a place known as "Kaleva", with its heroic figures; monstrous and wondrous animals ranging from fish and land animals to birds; a warm paradise called "Lintukoto"; a land of the dead called "Tuonela"; a wide variety of human magicians; artefacts ranging from wealth creation to music; and many others.

We have made preliminary arrangements with the Koroinen family. They are a Finnish clan of over a dozen members who live next to the prospective covenant site. In exchange for our help, protection, and compensation, the family has promised us their labour. To begin with, they and their neighbors will erect modest structures for our laboratories and sancta during the year 1219. Raising our covenant and its defences will otherwise rely either upon your Arts or sweat. Consider the site a canvas awaiting your brush.

The Koroinen family are accustomed to the Gift due to its presence amongst them, though this does not acclimatize them to us. The heads of the family, the elderly war leader Väinö and his witch wife Ilmatar, welcome our presence with surprising eagerness. Beyond this, the locals view us with suspicion, but also as a potential ally against entreaties by many a foe and danger. Any preparations, magical or mundane, to ease our acceptance would surely prove beneficial.

Mundane raids have been known to occur in the Spring and Summer. Furthermore, the abundant local magical traditions and supernatural creatures may not always view us favourably. Let us stand as the elm—rooted firmly yet flexible enough to withstand storms.

Should you wish to learn the local tongues—Swedish or Finnish— or know more about the area, I encourage you to journey to Oculus as soon as possible. We have excellent teachers ready, and I will count taking instruction in these languages as beneficial to the covenant.

Be mindful that the land is not as naturally bountiful or clement as in the South. I am confident that we will be able to sustain ourselves come winter, especially with the subsidy promised by the Rhine covenants, but any preparations or suggestions you can make for subsistence and comfort would surely be well advised.

We are fortunate enough to have substantial mundane resources from the benevolent covenants of the Rhine. Laboratories will be set up prior to arrival by my faithful apprentices, Helena and Rufus, who will travel to the site a year in advance. As you may have surmised from the slightly insipid style that characterizes parts of this letter, the hard-working wordsmith Helena has kindly assisted me in drafting it to ensure reception prepense. Our Finn-born translator Mieli has helped flesh out details on the local conditions.

Our library will mostly consist in damaged, though originally worthy, texts that were salvaged from the remains of the covenant of Weserufer. That covenant, as you are well aware, fell due to the feuds between its young and old magi. Let us mind the symbolism and steer clear of their fate. A scheme could surely be devised to repair the books.

I am wary of bringing over many mundane personnel until or unless we have ensured that the region can support an influx of mouths to feed and house. Rufus calculates that, notwithstanding your intervention, we can accommodate five to ten covenfolk to supplement the Koroinen family, who have promised to ensure our physical reproduction. I am planning on three shield grogs, a translator, and a scribe, as well as the usual wages and equipment for all. I am, however, open to interjections. Prepare for a scarcity of hands or find ways to alleviate it.

Our vis stockpile at the moment consists of ten pawns of Vim vis, and more is not forthcoming from the Rhine covenants. If one of the prospective members can cast the Aegis at the lowest level, the supply suffices for two years. If not, Gunhilda of Oculus has promised to cast it for ten pawns altogether, depleting our supply in a year. I have, however, a few promising leads on what I believe to be vis sources on the covenant grounds. I hope my apprentices will be able to gather their yield starting 1219 such that we can fund an Aegis, pay for projects, and pay back potential loans. Finding more should be a priority, however.

House Mercere has offered to lend the covenant, or individual members, vis for preparation at an annual interest rate of 20 percent (minimum one pawn of interest), and a repayment time of five years. During your year of preparations in 1219, you can count on the covenant taking on reasonably-sized vis loans that you need for preparations, insofar as I first approve the cost as beneficial to the covenant.

We will be visited by the Order's fleets twice a year. Oculus will arrive in early Spring — with the beneficence of Falke, whose ritual at Arkona thaws the Baltic each spring and opens the lanes — and Waddenzee grudgingly in early Autumn, before the ice closes again. These ships deliver our subsidies, if forthcoming, and enable trade. We must fend for ourselves during the remainder of the year, notwithstanding contacts with other traders.

The continuation and extent of our much-needed subsidies from Oculus, Fengheld, and Durenmar will be negotiated in the coming Tribunal meeting in 1221. More importantly still, our covenant status will need to be affirmed by every covenant in the Rhine. Our future is therefore contingent on diplomatic overtures. I beg you to cultivate allies, skirt enmity, and flex your bargaining muscles, lest our coffers run dry, library wither, and vis sources lay looted. I also ask that you inform me of any allies you have already secured, as well as let them know of your participation in this project.

Among the Order's venerable magi, our most important political ally is my beloved first parens, Gregorius of Tytalus in Fengheld, whose brainchild Caducus is. He has expressed a keen, if unexplained, interest in a hedge sorceress, whom we would do well to investigate. If we do this, Fengheld's support should be guaranteed. I expect Stentorius to remain indifferent unless we overtly disobey orders or break rules.

I persuaded Oculus to help us, but given that I must give up my membership in that fair covenant for Caducus, we will need to pad some purses to keep Henri and the others engaged with the project. The renegades of Waddenzee, by contrast, only agreed to act as our supply convoy after substantial pressure from Gregorius, which suggests they are likely to renege on their promise if the chance presents itself.

Our most precarious foundation, though perhaps most important, is Durenmar. The gimlet-eyed Philippus expects us to go native, build a base for rebellion, and upset all things sacred and sanctimonious – as opposed to striking against the Order's enemies in the proper manner. Happily, my second parens Murion assured a majority of her sodales that Caducus is a beachhead, an outlet, and a patch of virgin soil for the Elm, but any indications to the contrary may sway the balance.

As long as we manage to keep the support of several large covenants, the others are unlikely to dare deny us our status at Tribunal - unless gravely offended, of course. Regardless of this, some covenants have promised us their favor in exchange for services, for which we can also negotiate a reward to nourish our kindling flame. I will prepare a full list later, but some of the requests include producing trade goods and conducting inquiries. Prepare to fulfil such aspirations as best as you can, or invent other ways in which to prove our worth. In particular, many of the Rhine magi perceive us as either the youth's hope, appeaser, or staging ground, according to their respective political inclinations. A savvy magus might soothe all groups.

If, given the above, there is anything magical or mundane that you think we should bring along, I invite you to write to me as soon as possible with requests, as well as arguments defending them. While all wishes cannot be granted, each will be given thoughtful consideration as I assemble the expedition.

For the present, I stand as the sole formal member of Caducus, and you join as visitors—a condition honouring the wishes of our kind donors. The charter calls for two seasons of service of peregrinators, which will be dedicated to establishing us at the site and meeting our obligations to the Rhine. Upon fulfilling this, it is my intention, notwithstanding the unforeseen, to offer membership to the willing.

I have attached to this letter a charter draft for our new covenant. I am open to suggestions, though remember that our donors' wishes and fears must be accounted for. I look to sign the charter in the Spring of 1220, after which further amendment will have to follow the stipulations of the charter itself.

The charter appoints six salaried Officers who each control a specific aspect of covenant life, namely accounting, magical resources, internal organization, income, defense, and external relations. I ask you to express which office you would prefer to take on, as well as a brief project proposal, within its mandate, that you would like to realize for the common benefit of covenant. I will delegate the offices for 1220 based on my assessment of your suitability and the viability of your project. The distribution of offices will be properly voted on among members in 1221, at which point we will have a year of accomplishments to evaluate the candidates.

Due to pressing commitments, I will not accompany you on the voyage and will be present intermittently at the covenant site. Yet, like ever-watchful stars, my guidance and support twinkle from twinned quills generously donated by Durenmar at the behest of the ever-vigilant Philippus.

Lastly, my soror Lucilla of Tytalus has penned you a letter. While I respect her dedication to fostering growth among the young, I believe she may have interpreted my intentions too one-sidedly. I trust my actions speak more eloquently than her characteristically-abrasive words ever could. As a demonstration, I, too, have donated a copy of my work to the covenant. Murion added one of hers, as well as some interesting tomes on the area. Please weigh the costs of associating with the Birch Gild carefully, given the situation I have described above.

As we embark on this shared journey, may we find not only knowledge, but also the wisdom to use it well. The stream ahead is uncharted; what joy to navigate its currents together!

Yours,
**Aurelius**
